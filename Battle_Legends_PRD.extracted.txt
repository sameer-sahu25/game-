1. Executive Summary
Battle Legends is a stylized, third-person Roblox fighting game built around readable, skill-based combat, distinct original fighters, varied arenas and a repeatable progression loop. Players enter a social lobby, select a fighter, queue for a mode, battle other players or AI opponents, earn rewards and improve their collection. The product is intended to launch with a polished core experience before expanding into a larger roster, additional maps, ranked play and seasonal content.
1.1 Product vision
Deliver an accessible but deep Roblox combat experience with responsive controls, fair multiplayer rules, recognizable character identities and an environment that feels cohesive and rewarding to explore.
1.2 Product goals
Create a complete first playable experience with a lobby, at least one battle arena, character selection and functioning PvP combat.
Support core play on PC and touch devices, with controller support where practical.
Make combat server-authoritative and design the economy and progression to resist common exploit patterns.
Establish reusable systems for fighters, abilities, arenas, UI, rewards and game modes.
Provide a project structure and documentation that another developer can maintain and extend.
1.3 Non-goals for the initial release
A fully persistent open world or seamless MMO environment.
A complex esports ranking system, tournaments or guild warfare.
A large narrative campaign or cinematic story mode.
Guaranteed support for every device configuration or a fixed frame-rate target without device testing.
A promise that external models, audio or animations are included unless their files and permissions are actually available.

4. Core Gameplay Loop
Launch into the Battle Legends lobby and view the player's profile, currency and available activities.
Select or preview a fighter and review its role, statistics and abilities.
Choose a mode or enter matchmaking; the system assigns a suitable arena and spawn.
Fight using movement, basic attacks, heavy attacks, defense and character abilities.
Receive match results, statistics, experience and eligible rewards.
Return to the lobby to equip cosmetics, unlock content, practice or queue again.
4.1 Design principles
Combat clarity: attack start-up, active frames, hit feedback and cooldowns should be understandable.
Fairness: no client-controlled damage, currency, kill credit or match outcome.
Accessibility: basic actions should be easy to learn; advanced timing and positioning provide mastery.
Consistency: similar actions should have predictable rules across fighters, with character-specific differences expressed through tunable data.
Performance: effects and geometry must be scaled to supported devices and tested under multiplayer load.
5. Game World and Content
5.1 Lobby requirements
A navigable 3D hub with central landmark, Battle Legends branding, fighter preview stands, shop, leaderboard, daily reward area, training entrance and matchmaking portals.
Interactive stations must have clear prompts and keyboard, touch and controller-compatible interaction where supported.
Lobby ambience, lighting and subtle effects must not obscure navigation or cause unnecessary performance cost.
Players can inspect their profile, stats, selected fighter and available activities from the lobby.
5.2 Arena requirements
The first arena, The Eternal Arena, is a circular futuristic battleground with a central combat zone, concentric floor rings, glowing energy perimeter, four landmark towers, elevated platforms, decorative floating islands, fair spawn points and clear boundaries. Geometry must support combat readability and avoid unintended collision traps.

6.1 Character data
Each fighter has a stable identifier, display name, description, role, base attributes, ability references, animation references, effect references, unlock method and UI portrait.
Attributes are configurable and balanced centrally; avoid hardcoding fighter-specific values across unrelated scripts.
Selection, ownership and equipped state are validated on the server.
The preview supports rotation and inspection without granting access to locked characters.
Character assets and animation IDs must be verified; missing uploads must be explicitly identified as setup dependencies.
7. Combat and Controls
7.1 Combat feature requirements
Light attack chains, heavy attacks, character special abilities and ultimate abilities.
Configurable combo windows, recovery, cooldowns, damage scaling and knockback.
Block, dodge, dash, sprint, jump, landing and appropriate hit reactions.
Server-validated melee hitboxes, projectiles and area attacks, including range, line-of-sight and target eligibility checks where appropriate.
Clear attack telegraphs, hit feedback, cooldown indicators, damage feedback and audio-visual impact cues.
Reasonable counterplay: attacks should have readable start-up or other clear response windows; long combos must not become unavoidable defeats.

7.2 Combat authority and security
The client sends intent (for example, attack type); it does not declare damage, target validity, currency or match results.
The server validates character state, equipped fighter, cooldown, range, attack phase, target health and relevant match rules.
Remote requests are type-checked, rate-limited and rejected when invalid or excessive.
Kill attribution and rewards are computed server-side and guarded against duplicate processing.
Movement and ability effects must not permit unauthorized teleportation, speed changes or invulnerability.
Combat tuning is centralized and testable; exploit prevention must not rely only on client-side checks.

10. Progression, Economy and Rewards
10.1 Progression
Track level, experience, kills, deaths, wins, losses, matches played, streaks, achievements, owned fighters and cosmetics.
Award experience for completed matches and configured objectives; victory and achievement bonuses may be used.
Use configurable level thresholds and clear progress feedback.
Provide meaningful unlocks through play while preserving competitive fairness.
10.2 Battle Coins
Battle Coins are the primary earnable in-game currency.
Potential sources include completed matches, victories, legitimate kills, daily rewards and achievements.
All balance changes and item grants are server-authoritative.
Use configurable reward tables, transaction validation and duplicate-reward protection.
Show clear reward breakdowns and purchase costs.
10.3 Content and monetization
The shop may offer fighters, cosmetics, outfits, emotes, victory poses, visual effects and profile decorations. Optional Robux purchases must use supported Roblox purchase flows, server-side receipt handling and transparent disclosures. Avoid selling decisive competitive advantages. Any paid random-reward mechanic must be reviewed against current Roblox rules before implementation.
11. Data Persistence
Use Roblox DataStoreService for durable player progress, with safe loading, saving, validation and recovery behavior. Never replace a failed load with defaults and then save those defaults over existing data.
Persist currency, level/experience, statistics, owned and equipped content, achievements and suitable preferences.
Use protected calls, bounded retries, periodic saves where appropriate, leave saves and BindToClose handling.
Validate and normalize data; use schema versions and migration logic.
Make reward grants idempotent and protect against duplicate match completion processing.
Test persistence in a configured test environment and document Roblox Studio API access requirements.
12. AI, Training and Social Features
12.1 Training
Provide stationary, moving, blocking, dodging, combo and damage-test dummies.
Display damage and useful practice feedback.
Allow safe reset and configurable practice conditions.
Keep training rewards separate from competitive rewards unless explicitly balanced and validated.
12.2 AI survival (post-MVP)
NPC states: idle, patrol, detect, approach, attack, defend, recover and defeated.
Use PathfindingService when appropriate and throttle AI updates.
Offer Easy, Normal, Hard and Expert tuning through configuration.
NPC attacks must be readable and follow consistent gameplay rules.
12.3 Social features
Player profiles and match statistics.
Party invitations and friend-oriented matchmaking where supported.
Private server support and spectator mode as later features.
Use Roblox-supported reporting and moderation features; do not create unsafe off-platform communication.

14.1 Code quality requirements
Use readable Luau, strict typing where practical, consistent naming and comments for non-obvious behavior.
Separate services, controllers, configuration and presentation logic.
Use centralized definitions for fighter stats, abilities, cooldowns, rewards and asset references.
Clean up event connections, temporary instances and asynchronous work appropriately.
Use protected calls for failure-prone platform services and provide actionable diagnostics.
Do not reference nonexistent instances, undeclared modules or fabricated asset IDs.
Create an initialization/bootstrap path that checks dependencies and reports configuration errors.

18. Acceptance Criteria
The MVP is acceptable for internal playtesting when all of the following are true:
The project opens and initializes in Roblox Studio without critical errors.
The lobby and one complete arena are generated or loaded correctly.
At least two original fighters can be selected, spawned and controlled.
PC and mobile players can move, attack and use at least one special ability per fighter.
The server validates attacks, damage, cooldowns and kill attribution.
A complete free-for-all match can start, run, end and show results.
Players can respawn or return to the lobby according to the configured rules.
The HUD displays accurate health, match status and session statistics.
Invalid or excessive remote requests are rejected safely.
Core UI actions work, and unavailable features are clearly identified rather than presented as functional.
The game has been tested with multiple clients in Studio and documented limitations are recorded.
The first public release additionally requires:
Required fighter and map content is complete and tested.
Persistent data, reward handling and purchase flows are tested in an appropriate environment.
Mobile layout and performance have been checked on representative devices.
All external assets have valid IDs, permissions and suitable content.
Current Roblox publication, safety, privacy and monetization requirements have been reviewed.
A release checklist, rollback/update plan and player-facing game description are ready.

20. Final Product Statement
Battle Legends is intended to become a polished, expandable Roblox multiplayer fighting game with an original visual identity, a social lobby, carefully designed arenas, distinctive fighters, responsive and fair combat, accessible controls, meaningful progression and reliable technical foundations.
